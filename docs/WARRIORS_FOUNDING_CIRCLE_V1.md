# Warriors Founding Circle v1 — canonical operating model

Status: **founding pilot**  
Owner: Vlad Kuzmenko  
Public entry: `/warriors-team`, `/ua/warriors-team`, `/ru/warriors-team`  
Canonical lead intent: `warriors_team_application`

## 1. Product thesis

Warriors is not a content library, public community feed, course bundle, member directory or networking database.

The product is a **small, curated execution circle** where members can bring real decisions and bottlenecks, receive direct peer feedback, make useful introductions and be held to commitments by people who remember what they said they would do.

The first version optimizes for:

1. peer quality;
2. trust and confidentiality;
3. repeatable execution rhythm;
4. visible member contribution;
5. low operational overhead.

It deliberately does **not** optimize for member count, content volume or software complexity.

## 2. Founding offer

- **Price:** USD 49/month after acceptance.
- **Billing model:** month-to-month. No payment to apply.
- **Founding protection:** the founding rate remains while the founding membership stays continuously active.
- **First-circle cap:** 8 active members.
- **Platform:** private Telegram group + live calls.
- **Live cadence:** two focused 60-minute Circle Sessions per month.
- **Async cadence:** Monday Commit + Friday Proof every week.
- **Included:** direct peer feedback, accountability and mutually relevant introductions.
- **Not included:** 1:1 consulting SLA, guaranteed business outcomes, lead lists, courses or unlimited direct access to Vlad.

No public instant checkout in v1. Selection happens before payment.

## 3. Ideal member

A strong founding member:

- is actively building or operating a real business, product, content engine or serious skill;
- has concrete evidence of execution during the previous 30 days;
- can explain a real current bottleneck or decision;
- gives useful feedback and experience rather than only asking for help;
- can participate in the weekly async rhythm and two live calls per month;
- respects confidentiality;
- wants long-term peer relationships rather than a prospect list.

There is **no minimum revenue threshold** in the founding circle. Execution quality and contribution are the primary gates.

## 4. Reject / waitlist criteria

Reject or waitlist when one or more of these materially apply:

- the primary goal is prospecting or selling to members;
- the applicant is idea-only and cannot show recent execution;
- the applicant wants motivation/content more than peer accountability;
- the applicant cannot commit to the operating rhythm;
- repeated status-seeking, hostility or poor feedback behavior is visible during the fit conversation;
- confidentiality expectations are not accepted;
- a direct competitive conflict would make candid sharing unsafe for an existing member;
- group composition would become too concentrated in one role, niche or experience level.

Protected personal characteristics are never fit criteria.

## 5. Application data

The website application must collect:

1. name;
2. email;
3. Telegram/WhatsApp;
4. project/business + link;
5. what the applicant is actively building and current stage;
6. recent proof of execution (last 30 days);
7. what the applicant wants from the circle and what they can contribute;
8. availability for the weekly async rhythm and two monthly calls.

All website applications use `warriors_team_application` so the sales/intake system has one canonical source of truth.

## 6. Review rubric

Score each dimension from 0 to 2.

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Active execution | idea / passive | inconsistent | clearly shipping |
| Recent proof | none | weak / vague | concrete evidence |
| Contribution | consume only | plausible | clear useful contribution |
| Accountability fit | resists it | uncertain | actively wants it |
| Reliability / cadence | cannot commit | partial | reliable fit |

Decision guide:

- **8–10:** fit conversation with likely acceptance;
- **6–7:** fit conversation / waitlist depending on group composition;
- **0–5:** decline respectfully.

The rubric is a decision aid, not an automatic acceptance algorithm.

## 7. Access flow

`Application → Review → Fit conversation → Accept / Waitlist / Reject → Payment → Onboarding → Circle`

### Application

No charge. Application enters the existing website lead pipeline with the founding-circle context.

### Review

Check recent proof, contribution, schedule fit and conflicts before booking a call.

### Fit conversation

Target: 15–20 minutes.

Agenda:

1. what they are actually building;
2. current bottleneck;
3. what they want from peers;
4. what they can contribute;
5. cadence expectations;
6. confidentiality and no-selling rule;
7. mutual fit decision.

### Accept

Send payment instruction/link only after acceptance. Once payment is confirmed, send onboarding instructions and private Telegram access.

### Waitlist

Use when the person is strong but the current circle composition, schedule or direct competitor conflict makes immediate entry a poor fit.

### Reject

Keep the message short and respectful. Do not invent a reason that was not actually observed.

## 8. Onboarding

Complete within the first seven days after activation.

Member posts a short **Member Card**:

- what I build;
- current stage;
- strongest area I can help with;
- current bottleneck;
- 30-day outcome;
- useful connections I am open to making.

Then:

1. read and acknowledge Circle Protocol;
2. add recurring Circle Sessions to calendar;
3. make the first Monday Commit;
4. receive 1–2 relevant introductions only if there is genuine fit;
5. attend the next Circle Session.

## 9. Weekly async protocol

### Monday Commit

Template:

- **Outcome:** one result that matters this week.
- **Proof:** what evidence will show it is complete.
- **Blocker:** the one thing most likely to stop it.
- **Ask:** optional specific help needed from the circle.

### Friday Proof

Template:

- **Shipped:** what actually changed.
- **Evidence:** link, screenshot, sale, conversation, metric, artifact or other proof.
- **Missed:** what did not happen and why.
- **Next:** the consequence / next move.

No leaderboard. The goal is useful accountability, not gamification theater.

## 10. Circle Session — 60 minutes

1. **5 min — Wins / material changes**
2. **10 min — Previous commitments review**
3. **30 min — Two hot seats (15 min each)**
4. **10 min — Asks, useful introductions, decisions**
5. **5 min — New commitments**

Hot-seat rule: define the real decision first. Members ask clarifying questions before giving perspective. Advice should be grounded in direct experience when possible.

## 11. Circle Protocol

### Confidential by default

Member stories, numbers and problems stay inside unless the owner explicitly grants permission to share them.

### No unsolicited selling

The group is not a lead list. Commercial conversations are allowed only where both sides have a genuine mutual fit.

### Contribute before consuming

Every member should add signal through experience, feedback, useful questions or introductions.

### Direct, respectful feedback

Do not optimize for politeness or aggression. Optimize for useful truth delivered clearly.

### Execution is the scoreboard

Plans matter only when they change action. Weekly proof is more important than performance in the chat.

## 12. Pause / removal policy

A check-in is triggered when a member:

- misses two consecutive live sessions without communicating;
- skips the async rhythm for three consecutive weeks;
- repeatedly uses the group for self-promotion;
- consistently consumes without contributing after feedback.

Possible outcomes: reset expectations, temporary pause, or removal.

Immediate removal can apply to deliberate confidentiality breaches, harassment, fraud/scams or repeated unsolicited solicitation after warning.

No public removal drama. Handle it privately and document the decision.

## 13. Introductions

Before making an introduction, confirm:

1. why the connection is relevant;
2. what each person is likely to gain;
3. both sides are willing to be introduced.

Avoid mass introductions. A small number of high-fit connections is the product standard.

## 14. Metrics for the first 60 days

Track only metrics that improve the product:

- applications received;
- fit-qualified applications;
- acceptance rate;
- accepted → paid activation rate;
- 7-day onboarding completion;
- Monday Commit participation rate;
- Friday Proof participation rate;
- live-session attendance;
- commitment completion rate;
- number of useful member-to-member helps/intros;
- month-2 retention;
- voluntary referrals / nominations;
- top recurring reasons for decline, pause or churn.

Do not use member count as the main success metric.

## 15. Validation gates

Do not build or buy a dedicated community platform until at least one of these is true:

- more than ~20 active members require multiple circles;
- community administration exceeds ~2 hours/week consistently;
- members repeatedly need searchable resources/member profiles/event history;
- recurring billing/access control becomes operationally painful;
- Telegram materially reduces participation or trust.

If a dedicated platform becomes justified, evaluate Circle first for a premium branded professional community; Skool is a simpler alternative when courses/gamification become more important.

## 16. 30-day founding validation

A first circle is considered promising when:

- at least 6 strong-fit members activate;
- at least 75% complete onboarding;
- at least 70% participate in weekly Commit/Proof;
- live-session attendance averages at least 75%;
- members can name concrete decisions, execution improvements or useful connections created by the circle;
- at least 60% of activated members choose to continue into month two.

These are internal validation targets, not public outcome promises.
